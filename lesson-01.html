The LLM Training Pipeline

Lesson #1 · May 9, 2026 · 2 min read

The Lesson

The industrial training pipeline using Hugging Face Transformers solves four distinct problems with four distinct data formats:

  1. Pre-training — Raw text sequences, next-token prediction across the entire vocabulary. Teaches the model language and world knowledge.
  2. SFT (Supervised Fine-Tuning) — Instruction-response pairs. Critical difference: you only compute loss on response tokens, masking the instruction. This teaches the model to follow directions.
  3. PEFT/LoRA — Instead of updating all parameters, inject low-rank matrices: W' = W + BA where B and A are tiny matrices. Freeze W, train only B and A.
  4. Preference Alignment (RLHF/DPO/KTO) — The post-training phase where raw capability becomes usable behavior. DPO optimizes directly on preference pairs, skipping the separate reward model step.

Concrete Example

Here's how SFT data formatting differs from pre-training:

# Pre-training: raw text, loss on ALL tokens text = "The capital of France is Paris. Paris is known for" input_ids = [1048, 2341, 992, 3821, 1102, 441, 882, 331, ...] labels = [1048, 2341, 992, 3821, 1102, 441, 882, 331, ...] # same # SFT: instruction-response, loss ONLY on response input_ids = [INST: "What is 2+2?", RESP: "2+2 equals 4."] labels = [-100, -100, -100, -100, RESP: "2+2 equals 4."] # -100 = ignored

The -100 label tells the loss function to skip those tokens. This is why SFT models learn to answer questions instead of just continuing text.

Key Takeaway
The training pipeline is a funnel — each stage narrows the model's behavior from general language understanding to specific, controllable outputs.

Why It Matters

As an AI leader, understanding this pipeline helps you make architectural decisions: when to fine-tune vs. prompt, when LoRA is sufficient vs. full fine-tuning, and whether your use case needs alignment or just SFT.
